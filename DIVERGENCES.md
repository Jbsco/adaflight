# Divergences from Betaflight

adaflight keeps the operation order of Betaflight's code, so a host build of each gives the same binary32 results for the same inputs, and the vector checker compares them bit for bit (`DESIGN.md` D6). Each item below names a place where adaflight differs on purpose and states how the recorded vectors treat the difference. "The reference" means Betaflight at commit af0638839, the commit the vectors were recorded from. Items that fix a Betaflight bug link to a write-up for Betaflight in `docs/upstream/`.

- **Moving average sum**: The moving average recomputes its sum from the window on every update instead of keeping a running sum, which removes the rounding drift the reference accumulates. The measured distance from the reference is at most 256 representable values over windows of 1 to 32 samples. The declared tolerance is 512.
- **Normalized vector components**: A normalized vector saturates each component at one. The reference can return a component one representable value above one when rounding of the reciprocal root lands high. The declared tolerance is 1. The saturation did not trigger in the recorded vectors.
- **Sliding DFT twiddle table**: The sliding DFT twiddle table is static. The reference computes it at first use, and bin 18 carries a negative zero real part there, which a static Ada constant cannot express. The two zeros compare at distance 0.
- **Varint encoding of 128**: The varint encoder sets the continuation bit on every byte below the last. The reference emits the value 128 as a single bare byte 16#80#, which its own decoder treats as a continuation byte and rejects. The checker expects 16#80# 16#01# for that value and the recorded result for every other.
- **Low throttle TPA at a zero breakpoint**: The PID runtime's low throttle TPA multiplier is zero when the low breakpoint is zero. The reference divides by zero there and never reads the result. The checker expects zero wherever the recorded value is infinite or undefined.
- **PID filter initialization order**: The PID filters are initialized from the configured runtime constants. The reference initializes them before it derives those constants, so after a profile change its iterm relax lowpass keeps the previous profile's cutoff until the next initialization. At boot it initializes twice and the second pass reads the first. The checker records the second pass.
- **Hyperbolic TPA table**: The hyperbolic TPA table is sampled through the double-precision elementary functions of the Ada runtime where the reference calls the C library's single-precision power. Every recorded sample matches. A sample the reference leaves infinite or beyond the signal bound, where the curve's base cancels to zero at full throttle, saturates at the bound.
- **EZ landing mix range**: The EZ landing mixer adjustment divides by its limit factor to widen the recorded mix range. Where that factor is zero the reference's range is infinite. It saturates at the signal bound here. The motor outputs do not depend on it.
- **Averaged feedforward**: The averaged feedforward goes through the moving average above, so it carries that deviation. The checker holds it within 512 representable values or a hundredth of a degree per second, since the reference's running sum leaves a residue near zero. The RC throttle vectors keep the averaging off. In one of their sequences the reference's running sum kept a residue from samples above a million, which offset the average by 0.03 degrees per second (F233).
- **Narrower setting domains**: Two settings keep a narrower domain than the configuration interface admits: the hyperbolic TPA curve's two PID percentages exclude zero, where the reference's table is infinite or undefined, and the throttle minimum check stays below 2000, where the reference's throttle scaling divides by zero.
- **Failsafe deadlines across the counter wrap**: The failsafe compares its deadlines through the wrapping difference of the millisecond counters, where the reference compares the counters directly in three places. The two agree while the counter is below its wrap, which the recorded vectors stay under. Past the wrap the reference's deadlines invert for up to 49.7 days.
- **Barometric altitude power**: The barometric altitude takes its power from the double-precision elementary functions (D15), which land within one unit in the last place of the C library's single-precision power. The altitude subtracts the power from one, so near sea level the last bit of the power becomes up to 2 ** (-21) * 4433000, about 2.2 cm. The declared tolerance is 3 cm, and two samples of 6900 recorded needed it.
- **Battery charge integration**: The battery's charge integration for the analog and virtual meters multiplies the latest current by the microseconds since the meter was last serviced in 64 bits, where the reference's 32-bit product wraps on the first sample after a pack is detected, which spans the time since boot, and on every sample of a virtual estimate above 1073 A (finding F20). The recorded vectors keep that first sample and the virtual meter's scale small enough for the reference to stay in range, so the two agree on every step.
- **Automatic disarm deadline**: The automatic disarm deadline is compared through the wrapping difference. The reference compares the microsecond counter and the deadline directly, so a deadline set in the last seconds before the 71.6-minute wrap disarms at once, and one set before a wrap that the counter has passed does not fire for the next 71 minutes (finding F21). The recorded vectors stay clear of the wrap and replay bit for bit.
- **Runaway takeoff deadline**: The runaway takeoff trigger deadline is compared through the wrapping difference as well, where the reference compares the counter and the deadline directly (finding F24). The recorded vectors stay clear of the wrap and replay bit for bit.
- **In-flight adjustment limits**: The in-flight adjustments hold the feedforward gain at the configuration maximum of 1000, where the reference's step and dial adjustments reach 2000, and the horizon strength at or above the configuration minimum of 10, where the reference's selection reaches zero (findings F25 and F26). The recorded vectors keep both within the configuration bounds: the oracle starts the feedforward gains below 600, gives at most two feedforward ranges per sequence with low dial settings, and keeps the switch channels at or above 960 microseconds.
- **Negative RSSI**: The receiver's reported RSSI is held at zero where the inverted, scaled, and offset value is negative. The reference converts that value to an unsigned 16-bit count, which is undefined; the target's conversion saturates to zero, and the oracle's host wraps. The checker expects zero wherever the recorded value is above 32767, which no defined value reaches, and the RSSI percentage of that value as zero with it.
- **GPS rescue bearing**: The GPS rescue takes the bearing home from the double-precision arctangent of the Ada runtime rounded to single (D83), where the reference calls the C library's single-precision one. That function's last bit differs between glibc 2.39 and 2.44, and the recorded vectors carry the values of the host that recorded them, which the double route reproduces. The declared tolerance is 1 on the target heading and 256 on the error angle, the difference of two headings.
- **ExpressLRS signal to noise mean**: The ExpressLRS receiver's signal to noise mean holds its sample count at 2 ** 24 - 1 and adds nothing past it, where the reference's 32-bit sum of 8-bit readings overflows after that many packets without a telemetry response (finding F100). The recorded vectors stay far below the count and replay bit for bit.
- **Position navigation bounds**: The position navigation target holds its commanded velocities within 2 ** 60 metres per second, walks a target flown at a stated velocity within 262 kilometres of the origin, takes the vertical ramp's slew deceleration as 2 ** 100 metres per second squared where the remaining altitude error is below half a micrometre, starts a leg picked up with a chase velocity below a ten-billionth of a metre per second from rest, and holds the settle time at 2 ** 30 seconds, where the reference's floats run on to infinity or carry a vanishing velocity. The recorded vectors stay far inside every one of these and replay bit for bit.
- **Up and down reference symbol**: The up and down reference prints U or D for the zenith or nadir. The reference formats the symbol string's pointer with a character conversion and prints the low byte of its address (finding F110).
- **Mission item altitude**: A mission item's altitude converts to centimeters toward zero and saturates at the 32-bit range, where the reference's conversion and its sum with the home altitude are undefined beyond it. The recorded items stay within the range.

The recorded images never read past the region, and the checker takes the reference's extra bytes off its size.
